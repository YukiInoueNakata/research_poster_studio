# {#notice}

**Fictitious example.** All data, authors, affiliations, and the conference on this poster are invented. It is a bundled example of Research Poster Studio; only the three references are real.


# 1 Introduction {#intro}

## {#intro_l}

### 1-1. Background: a proverb about desperate busyness

- The Japanese proverb *neko no te mo karitai* (猫の手も借りたい) means "so busy that I would even borrow a cat's paws."
- Few situations fit it better than the week before a poster session.
- Posters assembled under time pressure often end up as a "wall of text" [@faulkes2023].

### 1-2. Problem: the proverb has never been tested

- The proverb assumes that **more paws mean more help**.
- Whether a cat's paw actually shortens poster production has not been examined.
- Our (fictitious) search of three databases returned **0 trials** of feline paw rental.

## {#intro_r}

### 1-3. Aims

We conducted a randomized trial of cat paw rental during poster production to test three hypotheses.

- **H1** Renting cat paws shortens production time.
- **H2** The effect grows with the number of paws (dose-response).
- **H3** Renting cat paws improves author morale.

> If the proverb is right, two cats (eight paws) should be the most efficient condition.


# 2 Method {#method}

## {#m_21}

### 2-1. Participants and design

- 60 graduate students preparing an A0 poster, and 30 cats from a paw rental agency (both fictitious).
- Students were randomly assigned to one of **5 conditions**: 0, 1, 2, 4 (one whole cat), or 8 paws (two cats); *n* = 12 per condition.

## {#m_22}

### 2-2. Procedure

- After a paw fitting and a short briefing, students had up to 72 h to finish a poster.
- Cats were free to help, sit on the keyboard, or sleep.

## {#m_23}

### 2-3. Measures and analysis

- **Production time** (h), **typos per page**, **author morale** (1-7), and **cat on-task rate** (% of observed time with a paw on the task).
- One-way ANOVA with polynomial contrasts; effect sizes follow @cohen1988.

## {#m_24}

### 2-4. Animal welfare

- Each rental lasted at most 72 h, and cats could leave the desk at any time.
- Every cat received treats on return; no cat was asked to type.


# 3 Results {#results}

## {#r_32}

### 3-2. Production time: a U-shaped curve

- Paw condition affected production time, *F*(4, 55) = 17.62, *p* < .001, η² = .56.
- The quadratic contrast was significant, *t*(55) = 6.11, *p* < .001.
- One or two paws shortened production compared with none (*d* = 0.58 and 0.72).
- Two cats lengthened it sharply (*d* = 1.96).

## {#r_33}

### 3-3. Typos rose faster than morale

- Morale rose with every additional paw, but typos rose much faster.
- Cats were on task less often as more paws were rented, falling from 41% to 9%.


# 4 Discussion {#discussion}

## {#d_41}

### 4-1. The proverb holds only up to two paws

- H1 was supported for one or two paws, and H3 was supported in every condition.
- H2 was rejected: the effect reversed beyond two paws, contrary to the proverb.

### 4-2. Why whole cats backfire

- Paws raise morale through warmth and purring, but whole cats also bring a body that lies on the keyboard.
- Beyond two paws, keyboard interference outweighs the morale benefit.

### 4-3. Limitations

- Cats were not blind to condition and did not give informed consent.
- Generalization to dogs, ferrets, and co-authors is unknown.
- All data are fictitious.


# 5 Conclusion {#conclusion}

## {#c_51}

### 5-1. A revised proverb

Borrow a cat's paws when you are desperate, but **stop at two**, and leave the rest of the cat at home.

- Authors who cannot borrow a cat may consult evidence-based guides to poster design [@faulkes2021].

### 5-2. Future directions

- A crossover design could separate the effect of paws from the effect of the rest of the cat.
- Testing *saru mo ki kara ochiru* ("even monkeys fall from trees") on senior researchers is planned.

> "So busy that I would borrow a cat's paws, but only two of them."
